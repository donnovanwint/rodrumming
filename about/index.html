About

I didn't leave teaching. I left the studio model.

A quick look at my background, my teaching philosophy, and the reasoning behind Rodrumming's fundamentals-first, video-only approach.

R

Background

I spent years teaching drums at a Music & Arts-style retail teaching center, working with students of every age and level in short, recurring in-store lesson slots. It's where I learned to diagnose technique problems fast and figured out which teaching habits actually stick — and which ones were just filling time on a schedule that wasn't built around the student.

Rodrumming is me teaching on my own terms: fewer students, more focused lessons, and a curriculum built around one goal — hands that move correctly and time that holds steady.

Why go independent

The studio model wasn't built for depth

Retail teaching centers run on volume — half-hour slots, rotating rooms, and a curriculum designed to keep students (and their instruments) moving through a store's catalog. It's a fine introduction to music, but it isn't built for a student who wants to actually fix a weak stroke or a rushing hi-hat pattern.

I found myself spending more time managing a room and a schedule than actually coaching hands. Going independent removed that friction entirely.

"I wanted to teach the way I actually think drumming should be taught — one student, full attention, and no rush to get to the next slot."
Teaching philosophy

Why fundamentals over full-kit playing

This is the core of how I've structured Rodrumming, and it's a deliberate choice, not a limitation.

Isolate the problem

A full kit gives a student four limbs' worth of places to hide a bad habit. A pad and two hands make every technique flaw immediately visible — and immediately correctable.

Build once, use everywhere

Grip, stroke control, rudiments, and timing transfer directly to kit playing, marching percussion, or any other drumming path a student takes afterward — none of it is wasted work.

Built for video, on purpose

Sticking and rudiments are easy to see and hear clearly over a video call. I can watch hand position, hear stick control, and correct in real time — no kit-sized camera setup required.

To be clear: these lessons don't include drum set or ensemble playing. Students who want that instruction are better served elsewhere — Rodrumming is specifically for players who want to build or rebuild their fundamentals on sticks and a pad, with the option to carry that technique onto a kit later, on their own.
